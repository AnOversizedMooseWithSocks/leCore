leCore — a memory you can watch

Type words. Each one is hashed to a frequency and a phase, and the field you see is the sum of those plane waves — that is fractional-power encoding, drawn instead of stored. Particles follow the field's gradient and repel each other, so they settle into the structure the words describe. No vocabulary is shipped and nothing leaves the GPU between frames.

Four passes per frame, none of which reads back: drift (one fragment per particle, summing plane waves for the gradient), splat (one point per particle, additive blending), diffuse (a five-point Laplacian ping-pong for the trail), and a colour blit. On an RTX A4500 those kernels measure 307×, 51× and 134× against NumPy — and the reason is that all three are per-element work with no BLAS equivalent.
